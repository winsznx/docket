Nothing to install.

Docket is a web page. Scan the code at the clinic desk and your ticket opens in the browser your phone already has.

Any phone with a browser

Android or iPhone, it doesn't matter. There's no app to download, no account and no password. Keep the page open, or come back to it from your browser's history.

Alerts by push, or by text

Tap Get alerts on your ticket and Docket sends a notification when you're close and again when it's your turn. Where the clinic offers it, you can ask for a text message instead.

On iPhone, add Docket to the Home Screen

iPhones only show alerts from web apps on the Home Screen, from iOS 16.4 on. It takes three taps in Safari.

  1. Tap the Share button.
  2. Tap Add to Home Screen.
  3. Tap Add.

Then open Docket from your Home Screen and tap Get alerts.

If you miss your call

You have {{graceMinutes}} to tap I'm coming. If you don't, you go back into the line {{reslotOffset}} places from the front, {{reslotTimes}}. Miss again and the ticket expires, and the desk takes it from there.

Leaving the line

Tap Leave line on your ticket and confirm. Your place goes to the next person straight away.

What Docket keeps

Your initials or first name, and your phone number only if you choose text alerts. Both are deleted the day after your visit. Read the privacy page for the rest.

Type made for reading in the sun

Docket is set in Atkinson Hyperlegible, which the Braille Institute made for people with low vision. Big numbers and strong contrast keep your place easy to read outdoors on a small screen.
